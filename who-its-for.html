Who it’s for

Built for different lives, not one kind of learner.

Traveller 1 · Meera

Kannada for healthcare professionals

When patients and families are anxious, tired, unwell, or unfamiliar with English, even a few clear Kannada words can make care feel more respectful, reassuring, and human.

What may matter to you

  • Introducing yourself and building early trust
  • Understanding simple patient and family responses
  • Asking about comfort, pain, food, movement, and routine needs
  • Giving simple, respectful instructions and reassurance

What can open up

  • Warmer communication during everyday patient interactions
  • More confidence in clinics, wards, home visits, and community settings
  • Better connection with colleagues and local support staff
  • Less distance between professional care and human presence
Meera

“Each morning I walk the ward, and the silence between us and the patient feels so heavy.”

Anand

“A greeting, a question about comfort, a gentle instruction. Small words can close a very large distance.”

Traveller 2 · Arjun

Kannada for IT and workplace professionals

In a team that shares many languages, a little Kannada can turn colleagues into friends and a workplace into a community.

What may matter to you

  • Greeting colleagues and making small talk at the desk
  • Understanding everyday workplace phrases
  • Chatting with drivers, cooks, and local support staff
  • Handling daily commutes, errands, and neighbourhood life

What can open up

  • Warmer relationships with local colleagues
  • More confidence outside the office
  • A stronger sense of belonging in the city
  • Less reliance on English alone
Arjun

“My team chats in Kannada over tea, and I just smile and nod.”

Anand

“Start with the small things: greetings, times, a question about the plan. Soon you won’t only smile and nod.”

Traveller 3 · Sofia

Kannada for yoga learners and visitors

Yoga spaces are places of listening. Even a few Kannada words can help you follow, connect, and feel welcome.

What may matter to you

  • Greeting teachers and fellow practitioners
  • Understanding simple instructions and guidance
  • Asking questions politely
  • Sharing a few words about your practice

What can open up

  • Deeper connection with teachers and the local community
  • Greater ease during classes and retreats
  • Respectful, warm conversations
  • A richer experience of the place where you practise
Sofia

“In class I follow the poses, but I miss the words around them.”

Anand

“A few Kannada words (breathe in, breathe out, yes) can bring you closer to your teacher and the place.”

Traveller 4 · Neha and family

Kannada for residents, families, and everyday learners

Home is where language lives. Kannada helps you greet neighbours, support your children, and take part in the life of the place you live.

What may matter to you

  • Greeting neighbours and building friendly routines
  • Talking with shopkeepers, auto drivers, and helpers
  • Supporting children who hear Kannada at school
  • Joining festivals and community moments

What can open up

  • Warmer ties with neighbours
  • Confidence in daily errands and conversations
  • A shared language at home and in school life
  • Feeling more rooted in the community
Neha

“My daughter hears Kannada at school, and I can’t answer her questions.”

Anand

“Then begin with the lane outside your door. Neighbours make the best teachers.”
