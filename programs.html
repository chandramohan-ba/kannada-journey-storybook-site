Six-program pathway

A six-program pathway from first spoken sentences to confident, expressive Kannada.

Each program has its own practical outcome. Learners may enrol in one or more programs, while higher programs require completion of the preceding foundation.

Meet Anand, the teacher, and Rahul, the traveller. Turn the page and follow their journey, one chapter at a time.

Program overview

Programs 1–2 focus on first spoken sentences and everyday use; Programs 3–4 introduce how Kannada works and how to express actions clearly; Programs 5–6 develop richer conversations and real-world expression.

  1. Program 1 Start speaking: simple, everyday sentences, and the Kannada letters.
  2. Program 2 Use Kannada everywhere: talk about anyone, anything, yesterday and tomorrow.
  3. Program 3 Understand how it really works: how speech turns into correct writing.
  4. Program 4 Say what is happening now, what is finished, and what you have experienced.
  5. Program 5 Complete the toolkit: longer, richer sentences for speaking and writing.
  6. Program 6 Grow your words and perform: vocabulary through themes, news, films and acting.

Six chapters. One road. Each chapter leaves Rahul able to do something real.

Program 1 · Kannada First Steps

What you will learn

Program 1 teaches by patterns first. You will not study how every word is built yet. Instead, you pick up sentences that people really say, and practise them until they feel natural. Reading and writing the letters grow alongside, but the main goal is to speak.

  • Read and write the Kannada letters, starting from the very first one, ಅ.
  • Say what you do: “I go to the office.”
  • Ask a question: “Will you go?”
  • Say what you won’t do: “I will not go to the office.” “I will not drink coffee.”
  • Politely ask someone to do something (“Please come”), or tell a friend (“Go!”).

By the end, you can

  • Read and write simple Kannada words and sentences.
  • Use words like “I” and “you” in everyday sentences.
  • Give a simple request or instruction, and ask a simple question.
  • Say what you will not do, and answer questions about it.
  • Speak in short, useful sentences about what you do, and what you will do.

Program 2 · Kannada in Context

What you will learn

Program 2 takes your Program 1 sentences and stretches them to everything around you. You still learn through patterns, not rule lists, so you can speak first and understand the reasons later.

  • Talk about anyone: he, she, they, we, an elder, a whole group. The sentence changes to match who you mean.
  • Use the small endings that show how things connect: to the house, from the house, in the house, with a friend, his book, “I gave it to him”.
  • Talk about yesterday and tomorrow, as well as today.
  • Ask, say, say “not”, and request politely, in all of these situations.

By the end, you can

  • Talk about people, things, places, who owns what, and who receives what.
  • Handle everyday situations: the market, the bus, a neighbour’s doorway.
  • Speak about the past, the present and the future.
  • Do all this without having to work out the rules behind it.

Program 3

Kannada with Clarity Understand How It Really Works

“From patterns to proper grammar.”

Understand why Kannada sentences are built the way they are, and how everyday speech turns into correct written Kannada.

Narrator Scroll

As the spoken word becomes comfortable, the traveler seeks the hidden logic of the script. The written language stands ready to act as a perfect mirror.

Anand

“Now that your tongue knows the paths, let us unlock the structure of the script. Written grammar isn’t a weapon to grade you—it is a map that reveals exactly why we say what we say.”

Rahul

“Ah! Seeing how the spoken and written forms connect makes everything click. The structure feels incredibly clear now.”

Spoken → Written

Same meaning, two ways of saying it

  • hogteeni → hogutteneI go / I will go
  • barteeni → barutteneI come / I will come
  • maadteeni → maadutteneI do / I will do
  • avru → avaruThey (or he, respectfully)
  • avananna → avanannuHim

Kannada is shown in English letters, so you can say it aloud.

Program 3 · Kannada with Clarity

What you will learn

Program 3 opens the box. You already speak in patterns. Now you learn why the patterns look the way they do, and how the same sentence is written in books.

  • How everyday speech becomes correct written Kannada: “hogteeni” (spoken) becomes “hoguttene” (written).
  • How a word changes when you say to, from, in, with or of, and how to build those forms yourself.
  • The “base word” of an action (like “hogu”, meaning “go”), and how the endings added to it tell you who is doing it and when.
  • Everything from Program 2, revisited with the reasons behind it.

By the end, you can

  • Build correct written forms of words and actions yourself, instead of memorising each one.
  • Tell the spoken form from the written form, and move between them.
  • Read and write more accurately.
  • Keep speaking naturally while you do it.

Narrator Scroll

With structural keys in hand, the student learns to weave time into language. Actions come alive in flowing arcs and finished experiences.

“A language has true movement when you can describe time. Let us expand your speech so you can effortlessly talk about things you are doing right now, things you have finished, and the experiences you’ve had.”

Now, finished, experienced

Three different stages of an action

  • Oota maadta iddeeniI am eating (right now)
  • hogta iddeeni → hogutiddeneI am going (spoken → written)
  • hogiddeeni → hogiddeneI have gone (spoken → written)
  • Mysuruge hogiddeeniI have been to Mysuru
  • Baagilu teredideThe door is open (someone opened it)

Kannada is shown in English letters, so you can say it aloud.

Program 4 · Kannada in Flow

What you will learn

Program 4 is about time and stage: is the action happening now, is it finished, or is it an experience you carry with you? You learn each in everyday speech and in careful written Kannada.

  • Things happening right now: “I am eating.” “She is reading.”
  • Things that are finished: “I have eaten.” “I have finished the work.”
  • Experiences, and what an action leaves behind: “I have been to Mysuru.” “The door is open.”
  • How these differ from plain “I eat” and “I ate”.
  • How casual talk and formal writing say these things a little differently.

By the end, you can

  • Say what is going on now, what is finished, and what you have experienced.
  • Use all of these in speaking and in writing.
  • Tell the difference in meaning between “I eat”, “I am eating” and “I have eaten”.
  • Read and write sentences that use them.

Program 5

Program 5 · Rich Conversations

What you will learn

By now your toolkit is strong, but real Kannada also has long sentences, formal writing and small linking words. Program 5 fills those gaps, through conversations, explanations, stories, letters, essays and passages like those in school textbooks. It is for speaking as well as writing.

  • Joining ideas into one sentence: “The man who came yesterday is my uncle.” “Because it rained, I stayed home.”
  • Small linking words that never change, like and, but, so and also.
  • How sounds blend when words meet: “mane” + “inda” becomes “maneyinda” (from the house).
  • Two words fused into one: “tande” (father) + “taayi” (mother) becomes “tandetaayi” (father and mother).
  • Saying the same thing in a different way: rewriting a sentence without changing its meaning.

By the end, you can

  • Understand and make longer, richer sentences in speech and in writing.
  • Use linking words, blended sounds and fused words correctly.
  • Rewrite sentences in other ways.
  • Speak naturally, without big gaps in the basics.

Program 6 · Unstoppable

What you will learn

Program 6 is where words take over from rules. You build vocabulary theme by theme, then live in Kannada: reading, listening and performing.

  • Word themes: numbers (1 to 100, first, second, third, and big numbers), time, dates, family, body parts, colours, directions, weather, food, shopping and emergencies, with the phrases that naturally go together and small practical tasks.
  • Real Kannada: newspapers and articles, news clips, film scenes, interviews and stories.
  • Doing: roleplay, acting, discussions, talking about your own experiences, reviews, reflections and short pieces of writing.
  • New words, sayings and proverbs, picked up from context.

By the end, you can

  • Use practical words across the important parts of everyday life.
  • Speak about real-life subjects.
  • Understand new words from the way they are used.
  • Take part in roleplays, acting and discussions.
  • Express yourself with more variety, confidence and range, in speaking and in writing, and keep your vocabulary growing.
